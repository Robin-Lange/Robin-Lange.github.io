Research direction

Learning where it helps. Constraints where they matter.

Robots must make useful decisions when sensing is incomplete, models are imperfect, and operating conditions change.

I want to investigate how autonomous robots can recognize when their predictions or assumptions become unreliable, then adapt without sacrificing predictable behavior. My interests include learning-augmented planning and control, runtime safety supervision, and collaboration among robots with different capabilities.

A recurring lesson from my research is that better component-level accuracy does not automatically lead to better closed-loop behavior. I’m interested in methods that can be tested rigorously in simulation and on physical robots, with explicit attention to their operating limits and failure modes.

Control Honors thesis · In progress

Prediction under uncertainty

Reliable prediction for supervised control

Developed a physics-based forecast, a bounded GRU correction, and a reliability gate using past prediction errors. To compare their effects fairly, I kept the MPC and independent stopping supervisor fixed. Across 102 matched lead-vehicle profiles and 408 AVL simulation runs, learned preview improved the predefined closed-loop score by 1.82% relative to physical preview, short of the 2% target. The original reliability gate increased cost by 1.04% relative to a matched fixed blend.

A later 12-profile pilot of a refitted gate achieved the lowest mean jerk among eight policies, but sacrificed spacing performance and did not meet its acceptance criterion. Better forecasts did not reliably yield better closed-loop control.

Sensor-based validation

Truck-underride detection and test targets

Independently designed and built a modular, collapsible truck-rear soft target for sensor-based pre-crash and underride testing at CARISSMA. Compared its camera, LiDAR, and radar signatures with measurements of real trucks to evaluate the fidelity of a compact test surrogate.

The target supported camera and LiDAR evaluation but did not reproduce important under-trailer radar scattering. I also supported crash-inevitability analysis and am continuing soft-target research toward a first-author manuscript in preparation.

I trained and evaluated YOLO-based truck-rear and underride-guard detection models. KICSAFe's broader research investigates early recognition of unavoidable collisions and complex truck-underbody geometries.

Autonomous aviation

Mission AI for autonomous flight

Developed a hybrid state-machine and behavior-tree approach for mission AI in Fraunhofer IVI’s ALBACOPTER research. Explicit state-transition guards organized mission phases, while reactive py_trees behaviors handled execution and interruption. The implementation ran through ROS 2 Humble nodes.

Developed visualization and testing tools, benchmarked alternative architectures, and identified execution stalls and unintended transitions. I refined the transition guards and separation of responsibilities, after which my supervisor validated the framework in Fraunhofer’s flight simulator.

Mission AI · State machines / behavior trees · py_trees · ROS 2 Humble · Simulation validation
