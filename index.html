Robin Lange / Robotics & autonomy

Autonomy that meets the physical world.

I’m Robin Lange, a Mechanical Engineering Honors student with research experience in distributed autonomy, multi-robot coordination, and experimental validation of autonomous systems. I’m interested in developing planning and control methods that transfer across heterogeneous robots, particularly under uncertain or delayed information.

After a summer with IHMC’s National Center for Collaborative Autonomy, I’m continuing research at Georgia Southern University and am preparing for graduate research beginning in Fall 2027.

May–Aug 2026 IHMC Robotics

Collaborative autonomy

Multi-agent robotics at IHMC

Built a Rust/Python collaborative-autonomy platform around two Boston Dynamics Spot robots. Joint Activity Graphs supported group decisions; topic-based Zenoh communication let different agents share state. Capability-based task allocation and runtime safeguards supported heterogeneous teams.

Validated collection-and-delivery missions in more than ten hardware trials. For pickup, I paired coarse inverse kinematics with local perception. A manually operated Husky served as a mobile, raised drop-off platform.

Navigation Research & presentation

Visual SLAM

Indoor UAV navigation on embedded hardware

Integrated visual SLAM, mapping and navigation components on Jetson/RealSense UAV hardware. A 110-second cart localization trial yielded 14.5 cm RMSE on intervals with valid reference data; reference dropouts were excluded. Autonomous flight remains future work.

Sensor-based validation

Truck-underride detection and test targets

At CARISSMA C-ISAFE, investigated how a soft truck-rear target appeared to radar, LiDAR and camera sensors. Qualitative LiDAR and 4D-radar analysis informed refinements to its tarpaulin covering and geometry. I built the quick-assembly and fold-away mechanisms from an earlier concept.
